Intrinsic Labs is one founder with client projects, product bets and a research program. Most of the engineering and administration is done by AI agents. The system that makes that workable has three parts.

<SystemCards />

<h2 id="runtime">IntrinsicOS, the runtime</h2>

IntrinsicOS is a small program that runs continuously on one Mac. It does not do the work itself; it decides which agent sessions may start, starts them, stops them and records what happened. Its loop runs once a minute and makes no model call. Every model call it makes is a separately recorded session, with a start, an end, a cost in time and a result.

**The unit of work is an outcome**: a written result with acceptance criteria, usually a few hours to a few days of agent work. Work reaches the founder in one of three ways:

1. He writes or dictates a note about a project. A drafting agent turns it into a draft outcome, a task for him, or nothing.
2. Once a week, a strategist agent reads each client project and product and proposes up to three outcomes.
3. An issue assigned to him in a client's issue tracker arrives as a card for him to route.

Nothing starts until he approves it. On approval he picks which agent owns the outcome and which code repositories it may change.

**An owner session** works in its own copy of the repository, on its own branch. A session runs for at most two hours and must record a checkpoint (what changed, what is next, what it is waiting on) at least every 25 minutes; the runtime enforces both, not the prompt. When the work meets its criteria, the owner marks it ready for review. If it needs the founder (a decision, a login, a physical action) it says so and waits.

**Accepting releases the work.** When the founder accepts, the same owner gets one more session to merge, deploy or send the result to where it is meant to end up, and records whether it got there.

**What an agent may do is set per project**, in one line the agents cannot change:

```
autonomy: gated
```

`locked` means changes go to a pull request for the founder. `gated` adds a review by a model from the other provider before anything merges. `open` merges once automated checks pass. A project with no line runs nothing. Some actions are never automatic at any level: spending money, sending anything to a client or the public, deploying to production and accepting finished work.

**Other rules the runtime enforces:**

- *Review across providers.* Work written by a Claude model is reviewed by an OpenAI model, and the reverse.
- *A stop switch.* One command, or one button in VISR, stops new sessions from starting; running sessions finish.
- *Pacing.* Each provider's weekly usage is spread across the business days; a session that would start over pace asks first.
- *Standing approvals.* Routine requests that match a rule the founder wrote (moving a date, filing a task for him) are carried out with a receipt he can undo for a week.
- *Self-update.* When a change to the runtime is merged, it updates itself between sessions, runs its own tests and rolls back if they fail.

<h2 id="visr">VISR, the review desk</h2>

Everything that needs a person reaches the founder as a card in [VISR](/products/visr), and nowhere else. He works through it mostly from his phone: approving drafts, reading a short brief and screenshots for finished work, editing a document in place, sending feedback by voice, and accepting or parking results. If something is not on a card, it is not waiting on him.

<h2 id="agents">The agents</h2>

| Provider | Roles |
|---|---|
| **Claude** (Anthropic) | The default owner of an outcome (Opus, at medium effort), which hands smaller pieces to Sonnet and Haiku. Writes the brief on each review card. Reviews work written by OpenAI models. Runs the nightly documentation audit. |
| **ChatGPT / Codex** (OpenAI) | Drafts outcomes from the founder's notes and runs the weekly strategist. Owns some outcomes. Reviews work written by Claude models. |
| **Grok** (xAI) | Three assistants in a team chat with the founder: administration (calendar, inbox, client email drafts), finance (invoices, receivables, cash) and IT (monitoring). They reach the runtime's records through an authenticated connector and file work for it; they do not write code. |

Which model fills which role is a setting, not code, and changes as the models do.

## Measuring progress toward autonomy

Our goal is a company that can carry work from an idea to its destination with far less day-to-day involvement from its founder, while meeting or exceeding his standard of quality. The [home page's operating measures](/#operating-measures) follow work along that path, each read from the runtime's records with its own window:

- **Approved work accepted** follows every outcome approved in a window to a fixed cutoff and counts how many the founder accepted. Accepted means he took a demo-ready result; it does not mean the work reached anyone.
- **Accepted work released** counts accepted outcomes with a recorded release (merge, deploy or send) within 72 hours. The release is the agent's own record, not an independent check that a user received it.
- **Agent time on clients and products** is the share of agent working minutes spent on client projects and our own products rather than on the system itself.

What we cannot yet measure is the founder's own time: a daily self-report failed in our first trial, and his desk time is not yet read automatically. Until it is, none of these figures is an autonomy rate.

## What is not released

The runtime's code and the company's records are private: they hold client work and one company's operating state. What we publish is how it works, the measurements and what goes wrong.
