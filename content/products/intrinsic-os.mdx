Intrinsic Labs is one founder with client projects, product bets and a research program. Most of the engineering and administration is done by AI agents. The system that makes that workable has three parts.

<SystemCards />

<h2 id="runtime">IntrinsicOS, the runtime</h2>

IntrinsicOS is a small program that runs continuously on one Mac. It does not do the work itself; it decides which agent sessions may start, starts them, stops them and records what happened. Its loop runs once a minute and makes no model call. Every model call it makes is a separately recorded session, with a start, an end, a cost in time and a result.

**The unit of work is an outcome**: a written result with acceptance criteria, usually a few hours to a few days of agent work. Work reaches the founder in one of three ways:

1. He writes or dictates a note about a project. A drafting agent turns it into a draft outcome, a task for him, or nothing.
2. Once a week, a strategist agent reads each project it is enabled for and proposes up to three outcomes.
3. An issue assigned to him in [Linear](https://linear.app) arrives as a card for him to route; the integration only reads from Linear.

Nothing starts until it is approved. The founder approves most outcomes himself and picks which agent owns each one and which code repositories it may change. A manager agent, the Superintendent, can open outcomes too, within the same pacing and limits, and its choices are recorded as its own.

**An owner session** works in its own copy of the repository, on its own branch. It runs for at most two hours and records a checkpoint (what changed, what is next, what it is waiting on) at least every 25 minutes. When the work meets its criteria, the owner marks it ready for review. If it needs the founder (a decision, a login, a physical action), it says so and waits.

## Gates and approvals

The safeguards that matter most, in order:

1. **Approval sets the scope, and acceptance is his.** An approved outcome lets its owner build, review, land and check the result where the outcome says it belongs. When an outcome says the work must wait for him before it goes live, as a change to this site does, the work waits on a review card with its preview and evidence until he accepts it, and his accept releases exactly that work. The only exceptions: a manager agent may accept work that cannot reach real users, and the runtime may accept work whose delivery it has checked itself. Both are recorded as theirs.
2. **Some things always need his separate approval.** Spending money, sending anything outside the company, new commitments to anyone outside it, and anything beyond the approved scope are never covered by an approval or an accept.
3. **Work is re-checked before every session.** Before each session starts, the runtime confirms the outcome is still approved, the owner is valid, its repositories are ones the project allows, and neither the outcome nor the project's standing direction has changed since the last session. If either changed, autonomous work stops until he continues it.
4. **Routine requests follow rules he wrote, within fixed limits.** Moving a date or filing a task for him can happen automatically when it matches one of his rules, with a receipt he can undo for a week. The code refuses any rule that would spend, send, publish, deploy, merge code, assign an owner or accept work.
5. **Runs are bounded and visible.** A stop switch prevents new sessions from starting. Weekly usage is paced across the business days. Sessions are capped in length and must checkpoint, and every session and decision is recorded.

Review across providers is part of how work lands: work written by a Claude model is reviewed by an OpenAI model before it merges, and the reverse. The runtime's code enforces the session checks, who may accept, the session limits, the stop switch and the standing-rule limits. Those waits before going live, and most cross-provider review, are instructions the agents follow; the runtime checks the review only when a task's pull request lands and when it verifies a delivery itself.

<h2 id="visr">VISR, the review desk</h2>

Everything that needs a person reaches the founder as a card in [VISR](/products/visr), and nowhere else. He works through it mostly from his phone: approving drafts, reading a short brief and screenshots for finished work, editing a document in place, sending feedback by voice, and accepting or parking results. Beside the deck sits a chat with the Superintendent, the manager agent, which can walk him through the cards and act on them when he asks. If something is not on a card, it is not waiting on him.

<h2 id="agents">The agents</h2>

| Provider | Roles |
|---|---|
| **Claude** (Anthropic) | The default owner of an outcome (Opus, at medium effort), which hands pieces to sub-agents. The Superintendent, which manages the company day to day. Writes the brief on each review card. Reviews work written by OpenAI models. Runs the nightly health check and transcript review. |
| **ChatGPT / Codex** (OpenAI) | Drafts outcomes from the founder's notes and runs the weekly strategist. Owns some outcomes. Reviews work written by Claude models. |
| **[Grok Bot](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview)** (xAI) | Three hosted Grok Bot teammates the founder works with in chat: administration (calendar, inbox, client email drafts), finance (invoices, receivables, cash) and IT (monitoring). They reach the runtime's records through an authenticated connector and file work for it; they do not write code. |

Which model fills which role is a setting, not code, and changes as the models do.

## Measuring progress toward autonomy

Our goal is a company that can carry work from an idea to its destination with far less day-to-day involvement from its founder, while meeting or exceeding his standard of quality. The [home page's operating measures](/#operating-measures) follow work along that path. Each is read from the runtime's records and shown with its count and window:

- **Approved work accepted** counts how much of the work approved since a start date has been accepted, leaving out the last three days. Accepted means the result was taken, by the founder or, for work that can't reach real users, by the manager agent; whether it reached anyone is the next measure.
- **Accepted work released** counts, among accepted outcomes that needed a release step, those with a recorded release (merge, deploy or send) within 72 hours. Work already at its destination when accepted is left out. The release is the agent's own record, not an independent check that a user received it.
- **Agent time on clients and products** is the share of agent working minutes spent on client projects and our own products rather than on the system itself.

What we cannot yet measure is the founder's own time: a daily self-report failed in our first trial, and his desk time is not yet read automatically. Until it is, none of these figures is an autonomy rate.

## What is not released

The runtime's code and the company's records are private: they hold client work and one company's operating state. What we publish is how it works, the measurements and what goes wrong.
