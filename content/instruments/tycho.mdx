Tycho starts from a problem Ghost exposes. Mining a person's past record tells you what they decided in situations that already happened. It says nothing about the trade-offs they were never presented with, and it goes stale the moment their thinking moves.

The design: keep a ledger of the model's *uncertainty* about the person — specific, named gaps. Generate short fictionalized dilemmas at the frontier of that uncertainty and present them as missions in a game. The person plays; the choices they make are structured elicitation data; the ledger entries close. The fiction is consented and disclosed — it is the means, not a trick — and one of the open research questions is what the fictional frame does to the answers (the observer effect, measured rather than assumed away).

Tycho is also the company's own decision surface: the real trade-offs a one-person business has to make are the raw material for the missions. That is what makes it an instrument rather than a toy — the calibration data comes from choices that had consequences.

Status: design conversations and a design-system canon exist; the source survey on eliciting judgment is complete; nothing is built. This entry will be updated when that changes.
