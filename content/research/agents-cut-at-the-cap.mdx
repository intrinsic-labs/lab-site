We run software agents in sessions that each own a piece of work: an agent works for a while, records a checkpoint (a short status saying where things stand), and is resumed later. That checkpoint is what a person reviews. A session that ends without one leaves its files in place but its progress unreported.

Between September 23 and October 13, 10 of 121 such sessions ended at exactly 60:00, the hard cap set by the harness (the software that starts, times and stops each agent session), with nothing recorded. They were 8% of sessions and 29% of the minutes (600 of 2,063). The files survived; the status report and the item on the reviewer's queue did not. In 8 of the first 9 cases, a later session on the same work completed normally.

## What changed

Four things, on October 13:

- **The agent sees its budget.** The prompt states the start time, the hard cap (raised to 120 minutes), and the rule: record a working checkpoint at least every 25 minutes and the final one before 110.
- **A harness hook nudges it.** A hook is code that runs after each of the agent's tool calls. About 24 minutes after the last checkpoint, the agent gets a nudge, repeated at most every 5 minutes. A `sleep` of 10 minutes or more is blocked.
- **A human is alerted at 90 minutes** with no checkpoint.
- **A session cut at the cap keeps its card** on the review queue instead of disappearing.

## After

Over the next 2.2 days, 0 of 47 sessions were cut at the cap. Two of them ran past 60 minutes (78.7 and 86.0) and would have been cut under the old cap, so part of the zero is the longer cap, not the checkpoint rule. The 90th-percentile session got shorter (58.8 → 35.3 minutes); the median got slightly longer (7.7 → 10.8). Of the 8 nudged sessions that finished normally, the median time from first nudge to checkpoint was about 8 minutes.

One session still failed. It sat waiting on a code review by another agent that had hung, ignored 10 nudges, and was stopped by a separate stall check at 86 minutes. Its last working checkpoint was recovered.

**The hook is a nudge, not a guarantee.** Telling the agent its budget and nudging it from inside the session kept 90th-percentile sessions near 35 minutes. The one session that stalled ignored ten nudges. If the agent complies, a 25-minute checkpoint caps what a cut can lose at one interval; if it doesn't, the stall check and the human alert are the backstop.

## Where this goes next

Rerun the count once the after-window matches the before-window in length, and find out why only one model family was cut, starting with how long its waits were.
