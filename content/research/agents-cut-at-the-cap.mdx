Our company runs on agent sessions that each own a piece of work for as long as it takes: an agent works for a while, records where things stand, and is resumed later. The record is what a human reviews. A session that ends without one leaves the work done but invisible.

Between September 23 and October 13, 10 of 121 such sessions ended at exactly 60:00, the harness's hard cap, with nothing recorded. They were 8% of sessions and 29% of the minutes (600 of 2,063). The files survived; the status, the review card and the thread post did not. In 8 of the first 9 cases, a later session on the same work completed normally.

## What changed

Four things, on October 13:

- **The agent sees its budget.** The prompt states the start time, the hard cap (raised to 120 minutes), and the rule: record a working checkpoint at least every 25 minutes and the final one before 110.
- **A harness hook enforces it.** About 24 minutes after the last checkpoint, the agent gets a nudge, repeated at most every 5 minutes. Foreground `sleep` of 10 minutes or more is blocked.
- **A human is alerted at 90 minutes** with no checkpoint.
- **A session cut at the cap keeps its card** on the review queue instead of disappearing.

## After

Over the next 2.2 days, 0 of 47 sessions were cut at the cap. The 90th-percentile session got shorter (58.8 → 35.3 minutes); the median got slightly longer (7.7 → 10.8). Of the 8 nudged sessions that finished normally, the median time from first nudge to checkpoint was about 8 minutes.

One session still failed. It sat waiting on a hung review, ignored 10 nudges, and was stopped by a separate stall check at 86 minutes. Its last working checkpoint was recovered.

**The prompt alone was not the fix.** An agent told about a deadline still waits on things. The hook turns the deadline into an interruption that happens inside the session, and the 25-minute checkpoint makes the hard cap cost at most one interval of unrecorded work.

## Where this goes next

Rerun the count once the after-window matches the before-window in length, and find out why only one model family was cut, starting with how long its waits were.
