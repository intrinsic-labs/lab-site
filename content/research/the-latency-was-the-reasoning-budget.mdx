In a model comparison round, Qwen3.8-27B ran at a default "high" reasoning tier and measured **38–256 seconds per item**, clustering near two minutes. Its predecessor did the same items in about 31 seconds. The natural read was that the newer model was too slow for a 3,800-item pass and should be dropped.

The person reviewing the numbers said it "smells like we haven't gotten the model working properly yet," and asked for the fair retest: same slice, same prompt, reasoning off.

He was right. **With reasoning disabled, Qwen3.8 was the fastest local engine in the round** — ahead of Qwen3.6 — and finished all 300 items in 70 minutes of wall clock. The latency had been the thinking budget, not the model.

Three things worth keeping from the retest:

**The tell that a knob took is in the response, never in the flag you sent.** `reasoning_effort` and `enable_thinking` are both accepted by the API without complaint whether or not the runtime honours them. What proves the setting landed is `usage.completion_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens` and the length of `message.reasoning_content` on the way back. All 300 reasoning-off rows came back with zero reasoning content; every row from the earlier runs has it. That is the audit trail, and without it "we ran it with reasoning off" is an assertion.

**"MLX is faster on Apple Silicon" is a rule of thumb, not a fact about your machine.** MLX quantizations for this model landed hours after the GGUF ones. Measured head-to-head on the same slice, the MLX 4-bit build ran ~40 s/item against the GGUF's ~29 — and worse, **it could not be made to stop reasoning**: the MLX runtime accepted both knobs and silently ignored them. Only the llama.cpp/GGUF path honoured them. The GGUF build also ships speculative decoding (the API confirms it in `stats.accepted_draft_tokens_count`), which accounts for most of its win. The round ran on the GGUF.

**A 4× headline is relative to its own default.** The earlier round had noted that dropping to `reasoning_effort: low` made one item go from 152 s to 34 s. That was one lucky item; over a wider run "low" still measured 38–256 s, tracking the length of the excerpt being classified. The 4× was real *relative to the default tier*. It did not make the model fast. Off did.

## Where this goes next

Latency was only half the question. Accuracy with reasoning off was measured on the same 300-item slice and has not been put beside these timings, so the trade the retest bought is still unpriced. That comparison is the next thing to run, and it settles something concrete: if turning reasoning off costs little in agreement, the full 3,800-item pass runs locally at the speeds above, on hardware we own. If it costs a lot, the report is that this engine is fast or accurate and not both, and the pass goes elsewhere.
