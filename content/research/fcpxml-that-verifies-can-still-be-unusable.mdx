We had a coding agent cut two short client videos in Final Cut Pro: a 90-second crowdfunding promo from an interview plus a 180-file B-roll archive, then an 80-second testimonial from one 28-minute interview. The agent transcribed and analysed everything locally, wrote the timeline as FCPXML, imported it, and handed it to a human editor for polish.

## Schema-valid is not native behaviour

Seven distinct failures passed validation and showed up only when Final Cut imported the file or played it back:

- music ducking written as `volume` did nothing; Final Cut wants `<param name="amount">`
- camera audio re-enabled itself unless set both `enabled=0` and `active=0`
- clip gain silently clamps at about +12 dB
- a still image as a bare `asset-clip` crashed the importer
- offsets in file-relative time instead of each source's own timecode
- one delivery landed in the wrong library
- subframe offsets were renormalised on import

What caught them: export the XML back out of Final Cut, diff it against intent, then decode the rendered movie and run speech recognition on the final mix. Final Cut has no scriptable export, so that round trip needs a person or UI automation every time.

## Correct, and hostile to the editor

The second job surfaced a different problem. Everything verified, and the editor still could not work with it: fades written as volume keyframes he could not drag, a logo that could not be extended, source footage he could not find in the browser. The fix was to author for the GUI: native fade handles, real Cross Dissolves, stills with handle room, full source clips in a browser event, alternatives as separate labelled clips.

And once the human has touched the timeline, **never regenerate from the plan**. Export his version, patch only the subtree you need to change, then prove that everything else survived. One revision removed 99 frames of dialogue from a human-polished cut that way, leaving every untouched structure identical after offsets.

## The numbers we have

The editor's polish still changed a lot. On the first job he doubled the B-roll (13 → 26 clips, median clip 4.0 → 2.2 s) using footage the agent never scouted. On the second, he cut a further 18% (82 → 67 s). The agent's music suggestions were all rejected.

## Where this goes next

The second job wrote 54 job-local scripts and reused one from the first. The next useful step is shared tooling, and a timed edit with and without the agent.
