## Where the human sits

If something is not on a card or in a thread in VISR, it is not waiting on Asher. These stay his:

- Merges on the files each project reserves for him.
- Production releases, sends to clients or anyone outside the company, spending money and deleting real data. The Superintendent can only ask for these, as a button he taps for that exact action, which it may then take once.
- Accepting work that involves interface or taste calls, client or public sends, production or a physical check.
- Anything beyond the scope he approved, and how much the Superintendent may do without asking.

The Superintendent may accept a finished result when a project allows it or the work cannot reach real users. Each acceptance is recorded with its reason and can be undone.

## What is still manual

- **Taste and the physical world.** Interface judgments are his, and an owner that needs a login or a physical action says so and waits.
- **Much of the review is an instruction, not a rule the code enforces.** The runtime enforces the checks before each session, who may accept, session limits, the stop switch that halts new sessions, and the merge rules. It checks for the other family's review only at some merges (sign-in and payment code, and an owner's work merged by the Superintendent) and when it verifies a delivery itself.
- **His time is not measured.** A daily self-report failed in its first trial, so the [operating measures](/#operating-measures) on the home page are not an autonomy rate.

## What has failed

- Over three weeks in September and October, 10 of 121 agent sessions hit a 60-minute limit before recording any status, 29% of all session-minutes. The limit is now 120 minutes, with a checkpoint at least every 25 ([write-up](/research/agents-cut-at-the-cap)).
- When many agents ran tests on the one Mac at once, four shared tools broke: a cleanup sweep, a time limit, a command wrapper and a version check ([write-up](/research/what-broke-when-many-agents-tested-on-one-mac)).

The runtime's code and the company's records are private.
