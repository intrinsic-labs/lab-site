## Where the human sits

If something is not on a card or in a thread in VISR, it is not waiting on Asher. These stay his:

- Merges on the files each project reserves for him.
- Sends to clients or anyone outside the company, spending money, deleting real data and production releases. The Superintendent can only ask for these, as a button he taps for that exact action, which it may then take once. An owner may release to production where its approved outcome allows it.
- Accepting work that involves interface or taste calls, client or public sends, production or a physical check. Other acceptance depends on each project's rules and how much he lets the Superintendent do; on a project he keeps for himself, it stays his.
- Anything beyond the scope he approved, and how much the Superintendent may do without asking.

## What is still manual

- **Taste and the physical world.** Interface judgments are his, and an owner that needs a login or a physical action says so and waits.
- **Much of the review is an instruction, not a rule the code enforces.** The runtime enforces the checks before each session, who may accept, session limits, the stop switch and each project's merge rules. For code, it checks for the other family's review only at some merges (sign-in and payment code, and an owner's work the Superintendent merges) and when it verifies a delivery itself. Edits to company documents go to that review automatically.
- **His time is not measured.** A daily self-report failed in its first trial, so the [operating measures](/#operating-measures) on the home page are not an autonomy rate.

## What has failed

- Over three weeks in September and October, 10 of 121 agent sessions hit a 60-minute limit before recording any status: 29% of all session-minutes. Per-outcome owners now get 120 minutes and checkpoint reminders; the persistent pilot owners have no hard limit ([write-up](/research/agents-cut-at-the-cap)).
- When many agents ran tests on the one Mac at once, four shared tools broke: a cleanup sweep, a time limit, a command wrapper and a version check ([write-up](/research/what-broke-when-many-agents-tested-on-one-mac)).

The runtime's code and the company's records are private.
